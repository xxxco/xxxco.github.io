Molecular epidemiology uses pathogen genomic similarity to infer infectious disease transmission patterns, enabling the identification of putative transmission clusters linked through direct or indirect infection chains. In HIV research, phylogenetic analyses have become an important tool for understanding outbreaks, risk factors, treatment resistance, and viral evolution in clinical and public health settings. Leveraging large public repositories such as GenBank and the LANL HIV Sequence Repository, this project integrates curated HIV-1 sequences with newly generated data to construct transmission phylogenies. Because HIV sequences deposited in public databases vary widely in length and genomic coverage, we systematically compared transmission clusters derived from commonly used sub-genomic regions to those obtained from complete genomes. Building on prior methodology [(Obeng et al., 2025)][Obeng], this work evaluates how genomic region choice affects cluster detection performance in real-world HIV molecular epidemiology studies.

[Obeng]: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.virol.2025.110558
