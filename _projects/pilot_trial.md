Clinical trials are commonly categorized using multiple overlapping paradigms—such as feasibility versus pilot studies, exploratory versus confirmatory trials, and phased development—making early-stage decision making challenging. Across application domains, a key goal of early trials is to assess whether continued development is likely to succeed, often framed as a go/no-go decision. In this project, I focus on pilot and feasibility trials and introduce a Bayesian approach to trial planning that directly quantifies the probability of meeting predefined success criteria. By leveraging Bayesian assurance, this framework provides a principled and interpretable way to justify pilot trial designs, particularly for estimating key design parameters such as dropout rates. To support practical adoption, I developed an R Shiny app that allows investigators to explore Bayesian pilot trial design using a simple binary endpoint, with extensible code provided for broader applications.

**Skills & Tools:** Bayesian Statistics · Clinical Trial Design · R · R Shiny · Statistical Decision Making

**Impact:** Enables more transparent and defensible pilot trial designs for early-stage clinical research.
